# Code of Conduct

> Part of the Open Walking Safety Helmet · Licensed CC BY 4.0 · © 2026 CLSOFTLAB, Dr. Lee Il-guk

We build this project for, and with, blind and low-vision people around the world. How we treat each other is part
of the work.

환영합니다. 괴롭힘과 차별은 허용하지 않으며, 문제가 있으면 아래 연락처로 비공개로 알려 주십시오.

## Our commitment

Everyone is welcome here, whatever their disability, vision, hearing, age, body, ethnicity, nationality, language,
religion, gender identity or expression, sexual orientation, education, income or level of experience.

**Blind and low-vision people are not only the users of this project. They are its experts, contributors and
leaders.** Their lived experience outranks assumptions made by sighted developers, including ours.

## What we expect

- **Be respectful and patient.** Assume good intent. Many people here write in their second or third language.
- **Make participation accessible.** Describe images and diagrams in text. Do not rely on colour alone. Use real
  headings and meaningful link text. Accept contributions by the means people can use: screen reader, braille
  display, voice input, or a helper typing on someone's behalf.
- **Talk about people, not conditions.** Ask how someone prefers to be described rather than deciding for them.
  Do not speak for blind people when they can speak for themselves.
- **Critique ideas, not people.** Safety debates can be intense. Keep them about evidence.
- **Take safety reports seriously.** Someone reporting a missed hazard or an injury may be shaken. Thank them first.
- **Respect privacy.** Do not share personal information, photos, faces or locations of others without clear consent.
- **Credit others' work.**

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, insults, threats, or deliberate intimidation, in public or private spaces of the project.
- Discriminatory jokes or language, including ableist slurs and mocking of disability, accents or language ability.
- Questioning whether someone is "really" disabled, or demanding medical details.
- "Inspiration" framing that treats disabled people as objects of pity or wonder rather than peers.
- Dismissing accessibility requests as unimportant.
- Unwanted sexual attention.
- Publishing others' private information.
- Pressuring anyone to test the helmet in unsafe conditions, or presenting the helmet as a replacement for the white
  cane or guide dog.
- Repeatedly derailing discussions or ignoring maintainers' reasonable requests.

## Scope

This code applies in all project spaces: the repository, issues, pull requests, discussions, chat, events, field
tests and user trials. It also applies when someone represents the project elsewhere.

## Reporting

- **Report privately:** open a private GitHub Security Advisory in this repository (Security tab → "Report a
  vulnerability"; say in the title that it is a conduct report), or contact a maintainer via their GitHub profile.
  If a report involves the project lead, contact another maintainer.
- Reports can be in English or Korean, or in another language if you need; we will find a translator you are comfortable with.
- Tell us what happened, where and when, and who was involved, as far as you can. You do not need proof to report.
- We keep reports confidential and share details only with the people who need them to act.
- We will not tolerate retaliation against anyone who reports in good faith.

## What happens next

Maintainers will review every report and respond as quickly as they can. Depending on what happened, actions may include:

1. A private conversation and a request to change behaviour.
2. A formal warning.
3. Removing content, or temporarily restricting participation.
4. A permanent ban from project spaces.

People affected by a decision may ask for it to be reconsidered by a maintainer who was not involved.
