## 1. The most important thing

**The helmet helps. It does not replace your white cane or your guide dog.**

- Always walk with your cane or dog, as you were trained.
- The helmet mainly warns about things your cane cannot reach: things at head height, and things
  moving toward you.
- If the helmet is silent, it does **not** always mean the way is clear.
- If you feel three very quick buzzes on your forehead, something inside is broken. Walk with your
  cane only, and ask for help to check the helmet.

## 2. What the helmet does

- Warns about things at **head height** in front of you. For example branches, signs, truck mirrors,
  open windows.
- Warns about **steps down, stairs, kerbs and platform edges** in front of you.
- Warns about **steps up** and low obstacles in front of you.
- Warns about **people, bicycles, cars and dogs moving toward you**, and says which side.
- **Reads text** in front of you when you press a button. For example signs and labels.
- **Describes what is in front of you** when you press a button.
- Tells you when a **friend** you have enrolled is nearby.
- Sends an **emergency alert** to your phone if you hold a button or fall.
- Warns you when its **power is low** or it is **overheating**.

Why a helmet? Sensors can miss things. If you do bump into something, the helmet still protects your head.

## 3. Putting it on

1. Find the **buttons**. They are on the **right side**, above your ear.
2. Put the helmet on, with the buttons on your right.
3. The helmet sits level. The front edge is about two fingers above your eyebrows.
4. Close the chin strap. You should fit one finger between the strap and your chin.
5. Turn the fit dial at the back until the helmet does not wobble when you shake your head.
6. Check that you can feel the three vibration pads: left temple, middle of forehead, right temple.
7. Put on the headset. It sits in front of your ears and **leaves your ears open**, so you can still
   hear traffic.
8. Put the power bank in a pocket or waist bag. Plug in the magnetic cable.
9. Make sure the cable runs down your back. Never around your neck.

The magnetic plug is designed to come apart if the cable is pulled hard. That protects your neck.

## 4. Switching on and off

### Switching on

1. Plug the magnetic cable into the helmet. The helmet starts by itself.
2. Wait about one minute.
3. You hear "Helmet ready." and a reminder that the helmet only assists.
4. You feel the **ready** signal: a quick sweep **left, forehead, right**.
5. Now the helmet is working.

Each morning, before you leave, check:

- You felt the ready sweep.
- Press the square button (□) once. You hear the status report, for example "All systems working."
- There is no fault signal (three very quick forehead buzzes).

### Switching off safely

1. Press the **circle (○)** and the **square (□)** buttons **at the same time**. Press them within about
   one second of each other.
2. **Hold both for 5 seconds.**
3. You hear "Shutting down." and feel the sweep **backwards: right, forehead, left**.
4. About 3 seconds later the helmet is off. Then you can unplug the magnetic cable.

Good to know:

- While you hold both buttons, the helmet will **not** start an emergency alert and will not repeat a message.
- If you let go of either button before 5 seconds, nothing happens. The helmet keeps running.
- Please use this way to switch off. Just unplugging can, rarely, damage the helmet's memory card.

## 5. Vibration language

There are three vibration points:

- **Left temple**
- **Forehead** (middle)
- **Right temple**

Three simple ideas help you remember:

- **Forehead only** means something is **in front of your face**.
- **All three together** means **the ground** in front of you changes.
- **One side** means something is **on that side**, often moving toward you.

The stronger and faster the vibration, the more urgent it is.

### 5.1 Things in front of your face (forehead)

- **Slow, gentle taps**, about two per second.
  - Meaning: something at head height, **1.2 to 2 metres** ahead.
  - No voice.
  - What to do: slow down, pay attention.
- **Fast buzzing taps**, about five per second.
  - Meaning: something at head height, **0.6 to 1.2 metres** ahead.
  - Voice: "Obstacle ahead."
  - What to do: slow down, check with your cane or hand, change direction.
- **Buzz that does not stop**, strong.
  - Meaning: something **very close**, less than 0.6 metres.
  - Voice: "Stop."
  - What to do: **stop now.** Check with your hand.
  - Note: when something is closer than about 20 centimetres, the sensor cannot measure it. The buzz
    continues for half a second and then stops. Stopping buzz does not mean it has gone.

Think of it like a car parking sensor: the closer, the faster, until it becomes continuous.

### 5.2 The ground in front of you

- **Two long, strong buzzes on all three points.**
  - Meaning: **step down, stairs down, kerb or edge** ahead.
  - Voice: "Step down ahead." or, for a bigger drop, "Drop ahead."
  - What to do: **stop. Find the edge with your cane.**
  - Note: very dark or wet ground can also cause this warning.
- **Three short taps on the forehead.**
  - Meaning: **step up** or a low obstacle ahead.
  - Voice: "Step up ahead."
  - What to do: slow down, find it with your cane.

### 5.3 Something moving toward you

- **Three strong, quick buzzes on one side** (left, right, or forehead for straight ahead).
  - Meaning: a person, bicycle, car or dog is **moving toward you from that side**.
  - The voice names what it is and the side.
  - **Urgent**, with strong buzzes: for example "Watch out, bicycle on the right!"
  - **Earlier warning**, with weaker buzzes: for example "Bicycle approaching from the right."
  - What to do: stop or move away from that side, as you judge safe.

### 5.4 People you know

- **Two soft taps on one side.**
  - Meaning: an enrolled **friend** is on that side.
  - Voice: for example "Mina is ahead on the left."
- **Four fast, sharp buzzes on one side.**
  - Meaning: a person you asked to be **warned about** is on that side.
  - Voice: for example "Caution, Mina on the left."

### 5.5 System signals

- **One tiny tap on the forehead.**
  - Meaning: "Got it". A button press was accepted.
- **Quick sweep left, forehead, right.**
  - Meaning: the helmet is **ready**.
- **Quick sweep right, forehead, left** (backwards).
  - Meaning: the helmet is **switching off**.
- **A number of even taps on the forehead.**
  - Meaning: a **number**, for example a floor number. Count the taps. Up to 10.
- **All three points pulse once every second.**
  - Meaning: the **emergency alert countdown** is running. It lasts 10 seconds.
  - What to do: press **any button** to cancel if you do not need help.
- **Three very quick buzzes on the forehead, repeating every 10 seconds.**
  - Meaning: **something important is broken** (a FAULT): the camera, a distance sensor, the vibration motors
    or the motion sensor.
  - A voice tells you what failed, for example "Front distance sensor not working."
  - What to do: **do not trust silence.** Walk with your cane only. Ask someone to check the helmet.
  - Less important parts, such as face recognition or Bluetooth, are announced once by voice, without repeating.

### 5.6 Coming later

- **Slow pulses on the left or right temple** will mean "turn left" or "turn right". This is for
  navigation in a future version. Version 1.0 does not use it yet.

## 6. Buttons

There are three buttons on the **right side**, above your ear. Each has a different shape and number of dots:

- **Front: circle ○, one dot**
- **Middle: triangle △, two dots**
- **Rear: square □, three dots**

Every press you make is confirmed by one tiny tap on the forehead.

Ways to press:

- **Short press:** press and let go quickly, in less than about 1 second.
- **Long press:** hold for 1.5 seconds or more. For the square, hold 3 seconds.
- **Double press:** two quick presses.
- A press that is a little too long for short but too short for long is ignored. Just press again.

### Circle ○ (front)

- **Short press:** read text in front of you. Face the sign or label, keep still, then press.
- **Long press:** repeat the last message.

### Triangle △ (middle)

- **Short press:** describe what is in front of you. For example "2 people ahead, bicycle on the right".
- **Long press:** where am I. The helmet asks your phone for the location and says it.
- **Double press:** who is here. The helmet tells you if it sees enrolled friends.

### Square □ (rear)

- **Short press:** status report. The helmet tells you if everything is working, if the phone is connected,
  and if voice info is muted.
- **Double press:** voice mute on or off. See the note below.
- **Hold for 3 seconds:** start an **emergency alert**. The countdown starts while you are still holding.
  See section 8.

### Circle ○ and square □ together

- **Hold both for 5 seconds:** switch the helmet off safely. See section 4.

### About mute

- Mute silences less urgent speech, such as friend names and small notices.
- **Answers to your own button presses are still spoken** when muted: text reading, scene description,
  status report, where am I, and who is here.
- Warnings such as "Stop.", "Step down ahead.", power warnings and fault messages are **always spoken**.
- Vibration is never muted.

### Button table (same information as above)

| Button | Short press | Long press | Double press |
|---|---|---|---|
| ○ circle, front, 1 dot | Read text | Repeat last message | None |
| △ triangle, middle, 2 dots | Describe scene | Where am I | Who is here |
| □ square, rear, 3 dots | Status report | Hold 3 s: emergency alert | Voice mute on or off |
| ○ and □ together | Hold 5 s: safe shutdown | | |

## 7. Voice messages

The voice speaks through the open-ear headset. Urgent messages interrupt less urgent ones.

The exact wording depends on your language. These are the English messages.

### Warnings, always spoken

- "Stop."
- "Obstacle ahead."
- "Step down ahead." or "Drop ahead."
- "Step up ahead."
- Approach warnings, for example "Watch out, bicycle on the right!" or "Bicycle approaching from the right."
- A warning about a person you asked to avoid, for example "Caution, Mina on the left."

### Power and temperature, always spoken

- **"Power is low. Charge the battery soon."**
  - The power bank is weak or nearly empty, or the cable is poor.
  - Change to a charged power bank soon. The helmet may switch off without more warning.
  - Repeated at most every 5 minutes.
- **"Helmet is overheating."**
  - The computer is too hot, for example in strong sun.
  - Move into shade if you can. Warnings about moving things may come later while it is hot.
  - Repeated at most every 5 minutes.

### Faults, always spoken

- "Camera is covered or too dark."
- "Distance sensors still active." This means the head-height and step-down warnings still work.
- A part that stopped working, for example "Front distance sensor not working."
- When it works again, for example "Camera view recovered."

### Emergency, always spoken

- "Emergency alert in 10 seconds. Press any button to cancel."
- "Sending emergency alert to the phone."
- "Emergency alert sent."
- "Emergency alert cancelled."
- "Phone not connected."
- "Fall detected."

### Settings changed from the phone, always spoken

The helmet tells you when the phone makes it less safe, so it can never happen silently:

- "Voice info muted by phone. Warnings are still spoken."
- "Drop-off sensitivity set to low by phone."
- "Volume set to 20 percent by phone." (for any volume below 30 percent)
- **"New phone paired: …"** with the phone's name.

If you hear one of these and **you or your helper did not change it**, someone else may be connected. Tell the
person who looks after your helmet. See section 9.

### Answers to your button presses (spoken even when muted)

- Scene descriptions, for example "2 people ahead, bicycle on the right."
- Text that was read. Dangerous signs start with "Warning sign:".
- Status report, for example "All systems working." or "Voice info is muted."
- Where am I, for example "You are near …".
- Who is here, for example "No one I know."
- "Text reading is not installed.", if that feature has not been set up.

### Other messages (not spoken when muted)

- Friend names, for example "Mina is ahead on the left."

If you missed a message, long-press the circle ○.

## 8. Emergency (SOS)

### Starting an alert

There are two ways:

- **Hold the square □ button for 3 seconds.**
- **A fall.** If the helmet detects that you fell and are lying still, it starts by itself and says "Fall detected."

### The countdown

1. All three vibration points pulse once every second.
2. The voice says: "Emergency alert in 10 seconds. Press any button to cancel."
3. **To cancel:** press any button. The voice says "Emergency alert cancelled."
4. If you do not cancel, after 10 seconds the helmet sends the alert to your phone.

### "Sending" and "sent" mean different things

- **"Sending emergency alert to the phone."** The helmet is trying. It repeats the alert every 5 seconds
  until the phone answers. **Help is not on its way yet.**
- **"Emergency alert sent."** You hear this **only after the phone has confirmed** that it received the alert.
  The emergency screen is now open on the phone.

### On the phone

- The phone sounds a loud alarm and vibrates.
- It shows your location.
- It shows two big buttons: **call the emergency number** and **send an SMS to your guardians**
  with a map link.
- **Someone must press the button on the phone.** That can be you, or a person nearby.
  The phone app cannot call automatically in this version.

### If the phone is not connected

- The voice says "Phone not connected." every 10 seconds.
- The vibration continues for 60 seconds, and the status light blinks fast, so people nearby notice.
- Any button press stops it.
- Call for help out loud, and use your phone directly if you can.

### Please practise

Practise starting and cancelling an alert at home with your family or instructor. Tell your guardians
before you test sending a real SMS.

## 9. Phone app and pairing

The phone app is a web app that connects to the helmet by Bluetooth. It works with screen readers.

What it does:

- Connects to your helmet. The helmet's name starts with `OWSH-`.
- Shares your location with the helmet, for "where am I".
- Shows the emergency screen (section 8).
- Settings: language, voice volume, mute, and drop-off sensitivity.
- Settings: your country's emergency number and your guardians' phone numbers.
- A log of everything the helmet said, so you can read it again with your screen reader.

Phones:

- **Android:** use Chrome. The person who set up your helmet can add the app to your home screen.
- **iPhone:** Safari cannot use Bluetooth from a web page. Use the free **Bluefy** browser app. A native app is planned.

### Pairing your phone

The helmet only listens to **your** paired phone. The connection is encrypted. Other phones nearby cannot
control it.

1. Switch the helmet on.
2. If a phone was paired before, pair **within 2 minutes** of switching on. The first time, there is no time limit.
3. In the app, tap **Connect** and choose the helmet named `OWSH-` followed by four characters.
4. The phone asks **"Pair with OWSH-…?"**. There is no PIN. Choose **Pair**. Your screen reader reads the question.
5. The helmet says **"New phone paired: …"** with your phone's name.
6. Next time, the phone connects by itself.

To use a new phone, switch the helmet off and on, then pair the new phone within 2 minutes.

**If you ever hear "New phone paired" and you did not pair a phone,** tell the person who looks after your
helmet. They can remove the pairing.

Keep the phone charged and Bluetooth on. The helmet's warnings work **without** the phone. The phone
is needed for location and emergency alerts.

## 10. Charging and daily care

### Charging

- The helmet has no battery inside. It runs from the **power bank** in your pocket.
- **Charge the power bank every night.**
- Roughly, a 10 000 mAh power bank lasts about 3.5 hours. A 20 000 mAh power bank lasts about 7 hours.
  Cold weather and old batteries give less.
- The helmet cannot read how full the power bank is. "Power is low. Charge the battery soon." comes only when
  the power is already weak. **Some power banks switch off with no warning at all.** Then the helmet stops
  silently. For long days, carry a second charged power bank.

### Daily care

- Wipe the **camera window** and the two **sensor windows** at the front with a soft, dry cloth.
  Dirt, rain drops and fingerprints make the helmet less reliable.
- Do not put stickers, caps or hair clips over the front windows.
- Check that the cable and magnetic plug are not damaged.
- Do not leave the helmet in a hot car or in direct sun on a bench.

### Replace the helmet if

- It has had a hard hit, a fall onto its shell, or a crash. Even if it looks fine, the foam inside may be
  crushed. The modules can be moved to a new helmet.
- The shell is cracked, or the foam is dented.
- The helmet maker's recommended lifetime has passed.

## 11. Rain, heat, cold and night

### Rain

- The helmet is **not waterproof**. It is made to survive light rain.
- Rain drops on the front windows can cause false warnings, or the fault "Camera is covered or too dark."
- In **heavy rain**, fog or snow, the helmet becomes unreliable. Rely on your cane.
- After rain, dry the helmet at room temperature. Do not use a hair dryer or heater.
- Never put the helmet under a tap or in water.

### Heat

- In strong sun, the helmet's computer gets warm and may slow down. Warnings about moving things may come later.
- If you hear "Helmet is overheating.", move into shade if you can.
- Bright sunlight also shortens how far the distance sensors can see.

### Cold

- Power banks give less energy in the cold. Keep the power bank in an inside pocket.

### Night and darkness

- The distance sensors (head-height and step-down warnings) **work in the dark**.
- The camera **does not** work well in the dark. Warnings about approaching people and vehicles, text
  reading and face recognition may not work.
- In a very dark place you may get the fault "Camera is covered or too dark." The voice then says
  "Distance sensors still active."

## 12. What the helmet cannot do

Please read this with your family and your instructor.

- It **cannot replace** your cane, guide dog or O&M skills.
- The head-height sensor looks in a **narrow line straight ahead**. Things to the side, and very thin
  things like wires and thin branches, can be missed. Turning your head slowly helps.
- Things **closer than about 20 cm** cannot be measured. The warning stops after half a second.
- **Glass** doors and windows can be missed.
- It does **not** see low obstacles well, except as "Step up". Your cane does that job.
- It does **not** see things coming **from behind**.
- It can give **false warnings**, especially on dark or wet ground, in rain, or near shiny surfaces.
- It can **misread** text, and it can **mistake** one face for another.
- It does **not** know traffic lights or whether it is safe to cross a road.
- It does **not** give turn-by-turn navigation in this version.
- It cannot tell how full the power bank is. It may stop with no warning when the power runs out.

## 13. Learning with an instructor

We strongly recommend learning to use the helmet with an O&M instructor.

Suggested steps:

1. **At home, sitting down.** Learn each vibration pattern. A helper can use the computer simulator to
   play them.
2. **At home, walking.** Practise with a helper holding objects at head height.
3. **In a quiet, familiar place outside,** with your cane and a sighted helper.
4. **In busier places,** step by step, only when you feel ready.
5. Practise the emergency alert and cancelling it.
6. Practise the safe shutdown with both buttons.

Instructors: please share what you learn in a
[field test report](../../.github/ISSUE_TEMPLATE/field_test_report.yml).
It helps everyone.

- △ short: describe. △ long: where am I. △ double: who is here.

- Forehead fast taps: 0.6 to 1.2 m.
- Forehead continuous: **stop**.

- Forehead three short taps: step up.
- One side, three strong buzzes: something moving toward you from that side.
- One side, two soft taps: a friend.

- Sweep left, forehead, right: ready. Sweep right, forehead, left: switching off.

- **Always use your cane or guide dog.**
